    sylva.coreg¶

    sylva.coreg ¶

    Marker-free coregistration of scan positions in forests.

    Registers scans with no targets and no initial alignment, from the trees themselves.

    1. Per scan (prepare_scan): a raster terrain model gives height above ground; horizontal slices through the stem band are fitted with circles and linked into a stem map; locally planar points (stems, ground, logs) are kept for ICP and foliage is dropped. Tilted scans are levelled first, with the scanner's own attitude.
    2. Per pair (register_pair): reflective targets where both scans saw three or more, otherwise a global stem-map match (stem distances do not depend on the unknown transform, so a sorted pair table makes the search near-exhaustive) whose height comes from the two terrain models, refined by a robust point-to-plane ICP. A pair is accepted only if it fits over all points and above the ground, so the ground plane alone cannot confirm a wrong pair.
    3. Whole survey (coregister): accepted pairs are edges of a pose graph, each weighted by the directions its surfaces constrain, solved with a Huber kernel and outlier rejection; scans left over are retried against the combined registered survey; optionally all poses are refined jointly (refine_joint). The report lists, per pair, how far apart the same tree lands from the two scans, a quality measure that needs no ground truth.

    Scans can be held fixed at trusted poses, so new or badly registered positions join an existing project, and approximate poses (a RiSCAN SOP, GNSS) can serve as priors.

    Examples:

    >>> from sylva import coreg, read_riscan_project, riscan
>>> project = read_riscan_project("survey.PROJ")
>>> scans = project.with_scans(require_sop=False)
>>> result = coreg.coregister([p.rxp for p in scans], names=[p.name for p in scans],
...                           levelling=[p.levelling for p in scans],
...                           reflectors=[p.reflectors() for p in scans],
...                           approximate_positions=riscan.gnss_to_local([p.gnss for p in scans]))
>>> print(result.report())
>>> result.save("transforms.json")

    sylva.coreg.pipeline ¶

    End-to-end coregistration of a survey.

    The pipeline is staged, and each stage can run on its own:

    1. Per scan (prepare_scan): a terrain model, the stem map, and a planarity-filtered subsample for ICP. The expensive part, done once per scan however many pairs are tried.
    2. Per pair (register_pair): reflective targets where both scans saw them, otherwise a global stem-map match with its height taken from the two terrain models, gives a coarse transform that ICP refines; the pair is accepted only if it fits over all points and above the ground, and its terrain agrees.
    3. Whole survey (coregister_prepared): accepted pairs become the edges of a pose graph, each weighted by the directions its surfaces constrain, solved with outlier rejection; scans left over are retried against the combined registered survey, and optionally every pose is refined jointly.

    Every stage records its own quality, because the useful question is not "did it run" but "which scans can I trust". Stem matches take their height from the shared ground rather than from stem bases (vertical error dominates stem-based registration, as Tremblay & Béland 2018 and GlobalMatch, Wang et al. 2023, both found); edges carry the anisotropic information of their point-to-plane correspondences instead of an isotropic weight; outlier edges are rejected even while some scans are unregistered; and recovered scans are tied in by pairwise measurements. Scans can be held fixed at trusted poses, so that new scans are registered into an existing project; and approximate poses (a RiSCAN SOP, GNSS and compass) can serve as priors, refusing results that move a scanner implausibly far and placing scans that see too few stems.

    CoregConfig dataclass ¶

    Settings of the whole pipeline.

    ground_min_coverage class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    ground_min_coverage: float | None = 0.8

    Least fraction of the elevations from -30 to +5 degrees an azimuth of the scan must sample for the terrain in that direction to be fitted from it; see _refit_visible_ground. Tilted scans sample almost none in the azimuths along their tilt axis, upright scans all of them. Needs the scanner's position, so it applies to scans read from file or given origin. None fits the terrain from every return.

    icp_min_planarity class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    icp_min_planarity: float = 0.35

    Planarity gate on the ICP points of every scan; 0 keeps foliage, which costs accuracy and can trap ICP in a local minimum.

    icp_max_height class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    icp_max_height: float = 12.0

    Returns higher above ground (m) are left out of ICP: the upper canopy is the least repeatable part of the scene and the most moved by wind.

    use_reflectors class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    use_reflectors: bool = True

    Try reflective targets before stems where both scans saw at least min_reflector_matches. Far more precise, but they usually cover only part of a survey.

    trusted_reflector_matches class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    trusted_reflector_matches: int = 5

    A reflector match with at least this many targets, agreeing within trusted_reflector_rmse, is accepted even when ICP then fails its tests. Scans tens of metres apart share targets long before they share enough surface for ICP's fitness test, and so many targets cannot form a congruent pattern by coincidence. The ICP pose is kept if it stayed within reflector_tolerance of the targets', otherwise the targets' own. 0 always requires ICP to agree.

    min_match_inliers class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    min_match_inliers: int = 5

    Stem correspondences a coarse match needs to reach ICP. Screening only saves ICP time; ICP fitness decides, so this is permissive.

    max_match_ambiguity class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    max_match_ambiguity: float = 0.8

    A coarse match whose best distinctly different rival has more than this share of its inliers is ambiguous: both are refined by ICP and the pair is accepted only if one fits the above-ground points clearly better.

    max_match_rmse class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    max_match_rmse: float = 0.3

    Largest scatter (m) of a coarse match's own correspondences.

    max_coarse_stem_rmse class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    max_coarse_stem_rmse: float = float('inf')

    Optional cap (m) on the median horizontal disagreement of matched stems; off by default.

    height_from_ground class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    height_from_ground: bool = True

    Take a stem match's vertical offset from the two terrain models where both saw ground, not from the matched stems. A stem's height is its own scan's terrain height under it plus 1.3 m, and under understory or on a slope that terrain is least certain exactly at the stems, while the shared ground between two scanners is wide and well sampled.

    ground_radius class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    ground_radius: float = 30.0

    Terrain cells further than this (m) from a scanner are left out of height comparisons: far from the scanner, ground returns are sparse and grazing, and the terrain model there is mostly the understory.

    min_ground_cells class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    min_ground_cells: int = 50

    Shared observed cells a height comparison needs.

    max_ground_disagreement class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    max_ground_disagreement: float = 0.25

    A pair whose terrain models still differ by more than this (m, median over the shared ground) after ICP is refused: ICP slid vertically, or the match is wrong. None disables the test.

    max_pair_distance class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    max_pair_distance: float = 40.0

    Pairs whose approximate positions are further apart (m) are not tried. Scale it with the range: with a 30 m gate no pair more than 35 m apart ever registered (0 of 1687).

    screen_pairs class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    screen_pairs: bool = True

    Match every pair first and run ICP only on the survivors.

    max_pairs_per_scan class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    max_pairs_per_scan: int | None = None

    After screening keep only each scan's best N pairs; None keeps all.

    min_icp_fitness class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    min_icp_fitness: float = 0.04

    Fitness decreases with the distance between scans, since less of each scan is shared. On a VZ-400 survey with positions 30-35 m apart, scored against its reflector-based registration, every pair ICP placed with a fitness of 0.045-0.10 was within 10 cm of the reference, and a gate of 0.10 left 10 of its 14 scans unregistered.

    min_icp_fitness_above_ground class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    min_icp_fitness_above_ground: float = 0.03

    Fitness over source points above fitness_min_height. Ground is planar and fits under any horizontal shift, so on open sites it can carry a wrong pair past min_icp_fitness alone; stems, branches and logs are what fix the horizontal position. On the same survey, correct pairs scored at least 0.043 and wrong ones at most 0.012.

    stem_agreement_tolerance class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    stem_agreement_tolerance: float = 0.25

    If ICP leaves the matched stems this much (m) further apart than the coarse match did, the coarse transform is kept: ICP slid somewhere unrelated. Loose on purpose; tightening it rejected good refinements.

    max_coarse_to_fine_shift class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    max_coarse_to_fine_shift: float = 2.0

    ICP moving further than this (m) from the coarse solution diverged.

    recover_unregistered class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    recover_unregistered: bool = True

    Retry each unregistered scan against the combined registered survey: a scan can overlap several registered scans partly without overlapping any one of them enough.

    refine_multiview class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    refine_multiview: bool = False

    Refine every registered pose in one joint Gauss-Newton over the point-to-plane correspondences of every accepted pair plus the matched stems (sylva.coreg.refine_joint).

    refinement_max_shift class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    refinement_max_shift: float = 0.3

    A refinement moving any scan further (m) is discarded.

    information_patch_points class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    information_patch_points: float = 100.0

    ICP correspondences counted as one independent observation when weighting pose-graph edges (~sylva.coreg.plane_edge_information): neighbouring residuals share the error of the surface they sample.

    information_min_sigma class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    information_min_sigma: float = 0.005

    Floor (m) on the point-to-plane residual that weights an edge.

    max_prior_shift class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    max_prior_shift: float = 5.0

    With priors: results putting a scanner further (m) from its prior position are refused.

    max_prior_rotation class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    max_prior_rotation: float | None = None

    With priors: if set, also refuse results rotated more (degrees) from the prior. Off, since recorded headings can be wrong by any amount.

    workers class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    workers: int = 0

    Scans prepared and pairs registered at once; 0 picks from the cores and free memory (memory_per_worker_gb per preparing scan).

    riscan_filter class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    riscan_filter: str = 'none'

    Apply RiSCAN PRO's RXP import filter when reading (sylva.riscan.riscan_like_mask): "none", "current" (drops echoes within 0.5 m of the scanner, as a current RiSCAN import) or "legacy" (also the weak, isolated echoes the older conversion discarded, a fifth to a third of a scan).

    riegl_options class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    riegl_options: dict = field(default_factory=dict)

    RXP reading (build it from a RiSCAN export settings file with reading_options): min_range, max_range (m, from the scanner), min_deviation/max_deviation, min_reflectance/max_reflectance, min_amplitude/max_amplitude, echoes, library. None bound by default.

    min_points_per_scan class-attribute instance-attribute ¶

    min_points_per_scan: int = 1000

    Scans with fewer points are set aside rather than processed.

    sylva.coreg.stems ¶

    sylva.coreg.matching ¶

    Global coarse registration by stem-map matching.

    Aligning two stem maps is finding the rigid motion that best superimposes two partly overlapping 2-D point patterns whose points carry a diameter. It is searched exhaustively over pairs of stems, not sampled: the distance between two stems does not depend on the unknown transform, so a pair in one scan can only correspond to a pair at the same separation in the other, and a sorted pair table makes that a binary search. Two correspondences give yaw and translation, the right model for levelled scans; ICP recovers any residual tilt. The search runs in the Rust core.

    sylva.coreg.icp ¶

    Fine registration by iterative closest point, tuned for forests.

    Two departures from a textbook ICP (Besl & McKay 1992) matter:

    • Point-to-plane with a planarity gate. The point-to-plane cost of Chen & Medioni (1992) is kept to planar neighbourhoods (stems, ground, logs), the parts stable between viewpoints: foliage is a view-dependent mess whose normals are noise.
    • Robust, trimmed correspondences. Forest scans overlap only partly, so many source points have no true match. A Huber (1964) weight with an adaptive scale and a trim (as in the trimmed ICP of Chetverikov et al. 2002) phased in over the first iterations of each level keep them from biasing the pose without discarding the far points that carry the rotation.

    The solver is a damped Gauss-Newton on SE(3) with a capped step, over a coarse-to-fine voxel pyramid. The loop runs in the Rust core.

    sylva.coreg.reflectors ¶

    Retro-reflective targets: the strongest registration feature, where present.

    A target is a point, not a fitted cylinder, so it is located to millimetres, and three shared targets fix all six degrees of freedom where a stem map fixes four. Where scans saw targets they are tried before stems.

    sylva.coreg.posegraph ¶

    Multi-scan global optimisation.

    Pairwise registration leaves a survey inconsistent: going A->B->C->A does not return to the start, and the drift shows up as doubled stems. A pose graph finds the poses that best explain every pairwise measurement at once, spreading the closure error over the loop (Lu & Milios 1997).

    Nodes are world_from_scan poses; edges are measured relative transforms with an information matrix saying how far, and in which directions, each is trusted: an ICP edge's comes from its point-to-plane correspondences (plane_edge_information), so a pair matched mostly on flat ground holds height, roll and pitch firmly and the horizontal position loosely. The solver is Levenberg-Marquardt (Levenberg 1944; Marquardt 1963) on SE(3) with a Huber (1964) kernel and an explicit outlier pass, because in a forest a pairwise match can be confidently and completely wrong when two parts of a stand have similar stem patterns. The graph here is a container; the solve runs in the Rust core.

    sylva.coreg.refine ¶

    Joint multi-view refinement: every pose solved at once from raw correspondences.

    The pose graph sees each accepted pair as one relative transform, which throws away the geometry behind it. This keeps every point-to-plane correspondence of every accepted pair, adds the matched stems as horizontal constraints, and solves all poses together with a robust Gauss-Newton, the reference fixed, so that errors are spread by the data rather than handed from scan to scan. The solve runs in the Rust core.
