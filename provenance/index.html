    What is ported and what is original¶

    Sylva is partly a port. Some of its methods are other people's software translated into Rust, some are published methods implemented from the papers, and some were written for Sylva. This page says which is which, so that credit goes where it is due and so that a result can be traced to the method behind it. The copyright and licence notices for the ported code are in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md, and each ported function names its source in a comment above it.

    Ported from other software¶

    Written with that software's source as the reference: translated into Rust, restructured and in places modified. These are not new methods; cite the original.

    From What in Sylva
    lidR (J.-R. Roussel and contributors) segment_crowns(method="dalponte2016") and the logic of als.li2012 (translations of lidR's C++); the rules and defaults of the local maximum filter in als.locate_trees; the standard area-based metrics of als.metrics (stdmetrics: names, formulas, entropy, cumulative height percentages).
    AMAPVox (UMR AMAP) sylva.voxels: the ray-traced voxel grid follows AMAPVox, whose path-length and attenuation estimators, leaf angle distributions and laser specifications it uses.
    raycloudtools (CSIRO; Lowe and Stepanas 2021, Devereux et al. 2026) canopy.density_grid (Lowe et al. 2021); in trees.segment_trees, the form of the edge cost (edge length to a power over a power of the cosine of the bend), the lateral term and the per-tree scaling; in qsm.build_qsm, the power-mean section radius, radii in proportion to the length carried (a fork sharing its parent's cross-section by length) and the cap on a fork's summed cross-section.
    GBSeparation (Tian and Li) leaves.classify_leaf_wood(method="gbs").
    The CSF reference implementation (Zhang, Qi, Wan and Wang) The cloth update and constants of ground.classify_ground_csf.
    The Point Cloud Library The parameters and window sequence of ground.classify_ground_pmf.
    SciPy Two distance-transform routines used by the coregistration.

    Following another design, with its own code¶

    • sylva.als processes a catalogue of tiles in buffered chunks, the design of lidR (Roussel et al. 2020). The engine, the DTM, CHM, normalisation, retiling and trajectory code are Sylva's.
    • trees.segment_trees and trees.merge_branches take the least-cost-path approach of raycloudtools' rayextract trees (Lowe and Stepanas 2021; Devereux et al. 2026), with its per-tree height scaling and lateral term as options, and the form of its cost for bending a path. Seeding the trunk surface rather than its axis is the idea of Arbor (J.-R. Roussel, r-lidar).
    • qsm.build_qsm builds its skeleton from shells of path length split into their connected pieces, the level-set skeleton of Verroust and Lazarus (2000) and Xu et al. (2007), and fits each axis's taper by the Theil–Sen estimator (Theil 1950; Sen 1968). qsm.wood_points starts from the path-frequency cue of Vicari et al. (2019).

    Implemented from published methods¶

    Written from the papers, without another implementation as the source. The references page lists each method and where it is used: circle and cylinder fitting, registration (Kabsch, ICP), the watershed and crown outlines, gap-fraction and plant-area profiles, leaf angle distributions, waveform decomposition, and the allometric and interpolation methods among them.

    Ported from the authors' own earlier software¶

    The coregistration of scan positions (terrain model, stem maps, matching, pose graph), the RiSCAN PRO project reader and the multi-slice stem detector began as a Python package by the same authors and were translated into Rust, with tests that hold the two to the same results. The pulse-centric Shots model comes from the authors' earlier canopy code.

    Written for Sylva¶

    • Stem detection as it now stands (trees.detect_stems): the bark prefilter, the linking of circles across layers, and the arc of a circle measured from point to point, with its allowance for sparse sampling (the share of the circle covered is the earlier detector's).
    • In the segmentation: measuring an edge's bend from a direction between the vertical and the arriving path, joining the graph's components where the cloud joins them, seeding from the real points of the bark shell, the rule for the ground layer, the understorey sources as implemented, and trees.merge_touching.
    • In the QSMs: joining the pieces of a tree's cloud where they come within reach of each other, the linking of the skeleton through the points' shortest paths, the choice of axes by the points a subtree holds, the length a node carries as the measure of each axis's taper, setting aside sections off their own axis's taper, measuring the wood filter's paths as the length of path a point supports and growing the wood through wood-like points, the weighted cylinder fit, the anchoring of every tree of a plot on its measured DBH (qsm.build_plot), and the detection and meshing of buttresses.
    • Pulse data: the Parquet format for pulses with their misses, and the resolution of multiple-time-around echoes in RIEGL scans.
    • Whole plots and surveys: exact processing of a terrestrial plot in tiles, change between epochs, the fusion of terrestrial and airborne data, the scan-quality measures and the registration report.
    • sylva.synthetic: the tree, plot and scanner models used for tests and checks.
    • The benchmarks on the documentation's benchmark pages: the paired-run protocol and what it measured.

    Where a method on this page is used in published work, cite its source as well as Sylva.
