    Pulse data¶

    sylva.Shots holds laser pulses rather than points: an origin and a unit direction per pulse, and a CSR list of echo ranges. A pulse with no echo is a genuine miss and still says where there was free space, which is what ray-traced canopy metrics need.

    From RIEGL projects and ray clouds¶

    Pulse data (from .rxp or a raycloudtools ray cloud) enables ray-traced metrics that account for free space. RiVLib's point stream holds only the pulses that returned an echo; fill_missing puts the others back, each at the place it was fired. A RIEGL scanner fires at a fixed rate while its mirror sweeps the zenith at constant speed, one line per azimuth step, so within a line the zenith is a linear function of time: a time gap of n intervals between two recorded pulses holds n − 1 misses at the interpolated angles, and from the first recorded pulse of a line the sweep is continued back to the sky end at the measured angular step (and from the last pulse on to the other end). The dead time between lines is left alone. The pulse interval and angular step are measured from the data; the scan pattern of a RiSCAN PRO project supplies only the zenith limits, which is why ScanPosition.read_shots(fill_missing=True) is the way to do it:

    proj = sylva.read_riscan_project("RobsonCreek.RiSCAN")
pos = proj["ScanPos001"]
shots = pos.read_shots()                              # project coordinates (SOP applied)
zen, gap = canopy.gap_fraction_pattern(shots, shots.to_pointcloud().z - pos.sop[2, 3],
                                       pos.pattern, min_height=1.0)
print("PAI", canopy.lai_from_gap_fraction(zen, gap, "hinge"))

full = pos.read_shots(fill_missing=True)             # the echo-less pulses, in place
grid = canopy.density_grid(full, voxel_size=1.0)
dtm = ground.make_dtm(ground.classify_ground_pmf(full.to_pointcloud()), 0.5)
z, pad = grid.mask_ground(dtm).profile_above_ground(dtm, bin_size=2.0)   # PAD by height

    Shots files¶

    Shots.save("plot.parquet") is the native way to keep pulse data. It is a Parquet file with one row per pulse: scan (index into the scanner positions held in the file metadata), zenith, azimuth, range (a list of echo ranges, empty for a pulse that returned nothing) and one list column per echo attribute, zstd compressed in row groups of about a million pulses. A miss costs two angles instead of a far "sky" point with every point attribute, an echo is a range instead of three coordinates, and nothing has to sit 1000 m outside the plot. polars, pyarrow, duckdb and R arrow read it directly.

    shots = Shots.from_ray_cloud(sylva.read("rays.laz"))   # or pos.read_shots(fill_missing=True)
shots.save("plot.parquet")
Shots.file_info("plot.parquet")                        # counts, echo bounds, scanner positions
shots = Shots.load("plot.parquet")                     # or groups=[0, 1] for part of it
grid = voxels.ray_voxelize("plot.parquet", 0.25, ground_class=2)   # streamed, never fully in memory

    Angles and ranges are float32 (echo positions return to about 0.01 mm per 100 m; double=True is exact). Origins within origin_tolerance (1 mm) are merged into one scanner position without moving any echo, which removes the per-ray rounding noise ray clouds carry. Mobile and airborne data (more than 65 536 positions) store the origin per pulse as delta-encoded 0.1 mm integers, which costs a few bits along a smooth trajectory. On a 185 M-ray mobile ray cloud (97 % misses) the file is 1.1 GB against 2.4 GB of LAZ, and voxelising it peaks at 3.6 GB of memory instead of 37 GB.

    Gap probability profiles¶

    canopy.GapProfile pools scans into returns and fired pulses by zenith ring, azimuth sector and height above ground (Jupp et al. 2009). Each echo of an n-echo pulse counts 1/n, the equal weighting of Armston et al. (2013). From that it gives plant area profiles and a plot summary:

    from sylva import canopy, io

prof = canopy.GapProfile.empty()                    # 5-75 deg rings, 36 sectors, 0.5 m bins
for pos in project.positions:                      # upright scans
    s = io.read_rxp_shots(pos.rxp, shot_stride=4)  # every 4th pulse, all its echoes
    fired = canopy.fired_pulses_per_ring(s, pos.pattern, prof.zenith_edges, shot_stride=4)
    s = s.transform(pos.sop)
    xyz = s.echo_xyz()
    a, b, c = canopy.fit_ground_plane(xyz, centre=pos.sop[:2, 3], radius=25)
    prof.add_scan(s, xyz[:, 2] - (a * xyz[:, 0] + b * xyz[:, 1] + c), fired_per_ring=fired)
r = prof.report()
r["pai_hinge"], r["pai_linear"], r["mla_linear"], r["clumping"], r["canopy_height"]
r["height"], r["pai_hinge_profile"], r["pavd_hinge"]
    • Estimators (all effective, not corrected for clumping):
    • hinge: −1.1 ln P(57.5°), at the angle where G is close to 0.5 for any leaf angle (Wilson 1963);
    • linear: Jupp's fit of −ln P(θ) against tan θ, which also gives a mean leaf angle;
    • weighted: Miller's (1967) integral over the rings measured, assuming spherical leaves.
    • Clumping is the Lang–Xiang (1986) index over every (scan, azimuth sector) segment of the hinge ring. pai_hinge_corrected divides by it.
    • Fired pulses. RiVLib's stream drops the pulses that returned nothing, so fired_pulses_per_ring rebuilds the fired counts (in the scanner frame).
    • Every azimuth step fires one pulse per zenith line.
    • Pulses per line are counted on the downward lines (100–125°), where every pulse hits the ground and so none is missing.
    • A line on a ring edge is shared between the two rings.
    • Neither the nominal phi_count nor a percentile over all lines will do. The scanner fires about 1 % more than nominal, and the mirror's angles do not sit on the nominal lines, so line bins catch extra pulses. In dense canopy nearly every pulse returns, so a few per cent too many fired pulses read as gaps and cap the PAI near 3.
    • With shot_stride both sides are decimated alike.
    • Shots that already hold their misses (a ray cloud, read_shots(fill_missing=True)) need no fired_per_ring.
    • A pulse that returned nothing still leaves a record in the stream: a pseudo echo, or the scanner's own near-range echo 0.1-0.2 m out (a VZ-2000i writes one for every such pulse, 2.4 million in a rainforest tilt scan). The reader counts a pulse as recorded only from an echo beyond min_range, so these become misses where they were fired. The pulses the instrument's own body and mount block leave the same record and count as misses too; they point at the tripod, below the horizon (for a tilted scan, behind and beneath it). On a VZ-i scanner the echoes are assigned to their pulses by the MTA resolution first, where the shim is built: on the raw stream, an echo filed under the wrong pulse leaves a false miss, two per cent of a Robson Creek scan and not one of them corroborated by the tilted scan on the same tripod. Shots.fill_missing_from_timing runs the same reconstruction on shots already in memory that kept their gps_time, e.g. a RiSCAN export that kept every echo (one exported with a reflectance or deviation filter has lost pulses that were not misses).
    • Misses for voxels. Shots.fill_missing(pattern) knows only the shots, not their timing, and draws each line's missing pulses at random azimuths. The count per ring is right, so ring-averaged gap fraction is served, but a miss drawn through foliage that returned every pulse traces free space where there was none: over a Banksia woodland plot the voxel PAI came out 28 % low against the same pulses with their misses in place. Use the timing reconstruction (read_shots(fill_missing=True)) for anything spatially explicit.
    • Ground. fit_ground_plane fits a robust plane through the lowest point of each grid cell, after Calders et al. (2014). Heights are only used for the profile. Returns below the ground model still count, in the lowest bin: an upward pulse cannot hit the ground.

    On a simulated turbid layer of PAI 3, hinge and weighted recover 3.0 within 2 %, and the linear fit 3.0 for horizontal and vertical foliage (2.84 for spherical). Its leaf angle is 0°, 90° and 54° against 0°, 90° and 57°. On eleven TERN plots read from the raw RXPs, the per-scan hinge PAI matches pylidar-tls-canopy's for the same scans (see the canopy benchmark page).

    Upward-looking scans from a 1.5–2 m tripod do not see vegetation at or below the scanner. In low woodland (mallee, mulga) the hinge PAI is close to zero while photographs and voxels find 0.5–0.8.

    Hemispherical photographs and scan images¶

    A scan with its misses is also the hemispherical photograph the scanner took. canopy.hemispherical_photo projects every pulse through a fisheye lens, the zenith at the centre and north at the top, and counts per pixel the pulses fired and those that met canopy above a height; gap is then a gap fraction per pixel rather than the sky-or-canopy a thresholded photograph gives, and gap_fraction_zenith and lai read the rings off it the way photograph software does. hemispherical_photo_from_cloud renders the same view from points alone, from any viewpoint, each point painted with its beam footprint: that is the camera's photograph, and where the scan is sparse it over-reads the gaps, since it cannot tell a gap the scanner saw through from one it never sampled.

    A worked example on one position of a RiSCAN project, run on a VZ-2000i scan of a closed rainforest canopy:

    from pathlib import Path
import numpy as np
from sylva import PointCloud, canopy, io, riscan

D = Path("ROBSON_2023")
project = riscan.read_riscan_project(D / "00_raw/RobsonCreek.RiSCAN")
pos = project["ScanPos070"]

# 1. The position's pulses, with the ones that returned nothing recovered from
#    the timing. Bounding the fill by the scan pattern's zenith sweep stops it
#    filling sky the scanner never pointed at.
pat = pos.pattern
sweep = (pat["theta_start"], pat["theta_start"] + pat["theta_delta"] * pat["theta_count"])
shots, report = io.read_rxp_shots(pos.rxp, fill_missing=sweep, return_report=True)
print(shots.n_shots, "pulses,", report["n_misses"], "recovered misses")

# 2. Scan images, in the scanner's frame: zenith down, scan lines across.
#    Misses are NaN so the gaps show.
steps = (pat["theta_delta"], pat["phi_delta"])
shots.scan_image("range", steps=steps).save("scan_range.png", log=True)
shots.scan_image("reflectance", cmap="gray").save("scan_refl.png", vmin=-20, vmax=0)

# 3. Level the scan with its SOP and give every echo a height above ground.
#    On steep ground sample the plot DTM instead of fitting a plane.
level = shots.transform(pos.sop)
xyz = level.echo_xyz()
a, b, c0 = canopy.fit_ground_plane(xyz, centre=pos.sop[:2, 3], radius=25)
heights = xyz[:, 2] - (a * xyz[:, 0] + b * xyz[:, 1] + c0)

# 4. The photograph the scanner took. 4500 pixels is one pulse per pixel for
#    a 0.04 degree scan to the horizon.
photo = canopy.hemispherical_photo(level, size=4500, echo_heights=heights,
                                   min_height=1.5, value="reflectance")
photo.save("hemi_binary.png")                   # sky white, canopy black, ground grey
photo.save("hemi_gap.png", kind="gap")          # gap fraction per pixel
photo.save("hemi_refl.png", kind="value", cmap="gray", vmin=-20, vmax=0)
zen, gap = photo.gap_fraction_zenith(np.arange(0, 91, 15))
print("ring gap fraction", np.round(gap, 4), "PAI", photo.lai(), photo.lai("miller"))

# 5. The same view from the coregistered plot cloud, within 30 m of the
#    position. It also fills the cone above 30 degrees that an upright VZ
#    scan never samples.
view = pos.sop[:3, 3]
parts = []
for f in sorted((D / "04_registered/tiles").glob("*.laz")):
    c = io.read(f)
    near = np.linalg.norm(c.xyz - view, axis=1) <= 30
    if near.any():
        parts.append(c.xyz[near])
xyz = np.concatenate(parts)
plot = PointCloud(xyz, {"height": xyz[:, 2] - (a * xyz[:, 0] + b * xyz[:, 1] + c0)})
camera = canopy.hemispherical_photo_from_cloud(plot, origin=view, size=4500, footprint=0.03,
                                               spacing=0.02, min_height=1.5, max_range=30)
camera.save("hemi_cloud_binary.png")
camera.save("hemi_cloud_range.png", kind="range", vmin=2, vmax=30)

    22826727 pulses, 5067 recovered misses
ring gap fraction [   nan 0.0002 0.0004 0.0002 0.0002 0.0309] PAI 9.28 7.11

    The first ring is NaN because the upright scan does not sample it. Sky is a few pulses in ten thousand through this canopy, so the hinge estimate sits at its saturation and the Miller integral, which weights the rings, reads lower; the cloud rendering reads lower again (3.7), since gaps in the point coverage count as sky.

    • size of about twice max_zenith over the scan's angular step gives one pulse per pixel (4500 for a 0.04 degree scan to the horizon). Coarser pools pulses, finer leaves pixels no pulse passed through, grey in the binary rendering and NaN in gap.
    • Lenses: equidistant (r ∝ θ, what most analysis assumes), equisolid (r ∝ 2 sin θ/2, Sigma 8 mm and Nikon FC-E8), orthographic, stereographic. max_zenith sets the rim, to 180 degrees for a full sphere; north turns the image and mirror=False puts east on the right as on a map instead of the left as a camera on the ground sees it.
    • The cone above. An upright VZ scan never samples zenith angles under 30 degrees. hemispherical_photo_from_cloud is the way to a full sky: the coregistered plot's points seen from the position, a camera's photograph rather than the scanner's, with the tilted scans of the plot in it.
    • Ground. A pulse that returned only from at or below min_height is a ground pulse: terrain and understorey, and on a slope the hillside, which fills a sector above the horizon from a tripod. ground_as says how it counts: "gap" (default, as GapProfile and Jupp do, since the path to the return held no canopy), "canopy" (as a thresholded photograph reads a hillside), or "unseen" (left out, so the rings describe only the directions that reached the sky). The binary rendering draws ground dark grey whichever is chosen. Heights from the plot DTM beat a plane on steep ground.
    • zenith_azimuth() gives every pixel's direction, to mask or weight the image yourself.

    Shots.scan_image lays the pulses out on the scanner's own grid, one row per zenith step and one column per azimuth step with the sky at the top, and colours them by a field. The steps are estimated from the pulses, or given from the scan pattern. Recovered misses land where they were fired, so this works on shots straight from read_rxp_shots with fill_missing, whose misses follow the recorded pulses; grid="lines" lays them out instead as scan line by index along the line, exactly as recorded: range by default, or n_echoes, zenith, azimuth, any echo attribute (amplitude, reflectance, deviation), or an array of your own. Pulses that returned nothing are NaN and draw black, so the gaps show, and the image is a quick check of a scan's coverage, a registration's range noise, or a reflectance calibration. scan_lines finds the line starts from the zenith turning back against the sweep (with the pulse timing, when the shots carry gps_time, so that a fast scanner's stray out-of-sequence pulses do not start lines).

    s.scan_image("n_echoes", cmap="viridis").save("echoes.png", vmin=0, vmax=3)
s.scan_image("range", grid="lines").save("lines.png", log=True)

    Four colour maps are built in (viridis, inferno, magma, gray, each with an _r reverse); with matplotlib installed any of its maps can be named.
