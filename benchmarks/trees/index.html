    Tree detection and segmentation¶

    The detect → segment → prune pipeline is scored against manually segmented reference plots (per-point tree labels) as instance segmentation: a reference tree counts as found when its best-overlapping sylva tree has IoU ≥ 0.5; predicted trees outside the reference coverage are ignored. raycloudtools' rayextract trees (Devereux et al. 2026) output is scored the same way.

    Plot (points) F1, pruned at 3 m at 5 m trees found / reference point accuracy understorey taken raycloudtools F1
    Litchfield, savanna (8 M) 0.977 (0.972–0.980) 0.979 (0.972–0.984) 124.0 / 127 0.981 0.26 0.97
    Robson Creek, rainforest (9 M) 0.653 (0.641–0.667) 0.671 (0.654–0.687) 109.8 / 149 0.691 0.71 0.55
    Wytham Woods, temperate broadleaf (14 M) 0.861 (0.846–0.872) 0.863 (0.851–0.876) 149.2 / 180 0.893 0.33 0.73
    Ofental, conifer (15 M) 0.649 (0.646–0.653) 0.689 (0.678–0.707) 63.0 / 89 0.726 0.39 0.73

    Scored in November 2026 with the defaults of version 1.0 on the plot clouds and reference labels as kept beside the harvest benchmark (the basis arrays): ground by PMF (the protocol's choice; sylva ground defaults to CSF) on a 5 cm thinning with windows of up to 17 m, a 0.5 m DTM, stems detected and trees segmented with the defaults (a 3 cm graph), pruned at 3 m and at 5 m. The edge cost of the segmentation and the arc test of the stem detection were replaced after version 1.1.0; on the same four runs the present code scores 0.977, 0.665, 0.856 and 0.671, which is the same within the run-to-run range (below).

    Each sylva figure is the mean of four runs of the same plot, with the range in brackets: the plot as it is, with the thinning grid moved by a fraction of a voxel, with its points in another order, and rotated by 30° (next section). Understorey taken is the share of the points between 0.1 and 2 m that lie on no reference tree and carry a tree label all the same (lower is better; below). The raycloudtools column is one run, scored the same way. The table is without trees.merge_touching, which sylva trees runs after segmentation and which adds 0.001 to 0.007 to these figures (below).

    Earlier versions of this page gave one run with a 5 cm graph: 0.964, 0.591, 0.864 and 0.594. Those figures reproduce exactly, and as four-run means the 5 cm graph gives 0.968, 0.597, 0.854 and 0.603. The 3 cm graph, which is the default, is better on 13 of the 16 paired runs, by 0.057 at Robson Creek and 0.047 at Ofental, for about 1.6 times the segmentation time (52 s against 32 s at Ofental).

    One run is not a measurement¶

    Nothing in a result should depend on where the thinning grid sits, on the order of the points in the file, or on which way the plot faces. All three move it. Over seven runs of each plot with the 5 cm graph (two grid shifts of a fraction of a voxel, two shuffles, two rotations and the plot as given), F1 at 3 m spans:

    Plot F1 over seven runs reference trees found in all seven in some in none
    Litchfield 0.964–0.988 119 7 1
    Robson Creek 0.582–0.625 70 53 26
    Wytham 0.797–0.874 119 47 14
    Ofental 0.576–0.636 52 17 20

    The count of trees found is steadier than the trees themselves: at Robson Creek about 100 of the 149 are found in any one of these runs, but only 70 in every one, and a third of the plot's trees are found or missed according to a nuisance. These are trees whose best overlap sits near the 0.5 threshold, in crowns that two stems contest.

    The default 3 cm graph is steadier as well as better. Over the four runs of the table at the top it spans 0.008, 0.026, 0.026 and 0.007 of F1, where the 5 cm graph spans 0.012, 0.028, 0.045 and 0.022 over the same four; 122, 100, 135 and 57 reference trees are found in all four of its runs and 3, 19, 26 and 10 in some. So:

    • a difference between two settings of less than about 0.04 F1 on one plot, from one run each, is not evidence. Compare settings as paired runs (the same nuisance on both sides) over several nuisances;
    • the tuning notes further down, which are single runs, are kept as the record of what was tried and not as measurements of that size.

    An earlier version of this page said that the scores came out the same at three corners of the thinning grid. The three corners tested were whole voxels apart, which is the same grid three times, so the test could not have failed. The bridged graph (below) did remove the largest effect of the grid: without it Litchfield loses 0.039 F1 and ranges over 0.886–0.948, and the other three plots do not change.

    What the scores cannot see¶

    The reference labels trees. Shrubs, saplings, grass and litter carry no label, so a tree that also takes the vegetation around its base scores exactly as one that does not. Measured directly, as the share of a matched tree's points that have no reference tree point within 0.1 m (one run, 5 cm graph):

    Height above ground Litchfield Robson Creek Wytham Ofental
    0.1–0.5 m 0.87 0.69 0.74 0.73
    0.5–1 m 0.83 0.61 0.50 0.51
    1–2 m 0.54 0.64 0.43 0.33
    2–3 m 0.23 0.63 0.42 0.18

    Below a metre, most of what a tree is given is not the tree. Part of this is by design: under low_height (0.5 m) a tree keeps what lies within low_radius (1 m) of its stem, grass included. The rest is understorey that the least-cost paths reach from the stem.

    The understorey sources (understorey_height, off by default) are the setting that addresses it, and what they cost and buy is now measured on both sides (four paired runs, 3 cm graph):

    Plot trees found: off → 10 m F1 at 3 m understorey taken reference tree points left unlabelled
    Litchfield 124.0 → 121.8 0.977 → 0.968 0.26 → 0.09 0.1 % → 0.7 %
    Robson Creek 109.8 → 110.2 0.653 → 0.657 0.71 → 0.64 0.1 % → 0.3 %
    Wytham 149.2 → 146.2 0.861 → 0.851 0.33 → 0.22 0.3 % → 1.3 %
    Ofental 63.0 → 63.0 0.649 → 0.657 0.39 → 0.28 0.4 % → 0.5 %

    They release between 1.7 (Wytham) and 13 (Litchfield) points of understorey for every point they take from a reference tree, and they cost two trees at Litchfield and three at Wytham: small trees whose crowns the sources reach from below and which then fall under the pruning height. Costing the sources as a 5 m tree instead of 10 m loses the same trees. The default stays off, because a tree dropped from the table cannot be had back afterwards while low points can still be filtered; set understorey_height=10 where the lower two metres of each tree matter more than the last two trees in a hundred (stem models, understorey studies).

    What the November 2026 review found¶

    The segmentation was reviewed against these plots in November 2026, each setting as four paired runs. No defect was found in the segmentation and its defaults did not change; one was found in stem detection (the arc test, below) and fixed. What was learned:

    Where the trees are lost. In one run with the 5 cm graph, 5, 50, 31 and 32 reference trees are missed. Most have a detected stem and a crown divided wrongly, the tree losing its crown to a neighbour or taking a neighbour's: 4 of the 5, 47 of the 50, 18 of the 31 and 20 of the 32. The rest have no stem candidate (1, 2, 13 and 7) or one whose tree ended under the pruning height (1 at Robson Creek, 5 at Ofental). The missed trees are shorter than the ones found (9.2 m against 15.6 m at Ofental, 8.7 m against 13.5 m at Wytham).

    Leaning stems are not detected. Of the 13 Wytham trees that are missed for want of a candidate, 12 lean between 25° and 45°, beyond max_lean_deg (25). Raising it to 45 with link_radius=0.3 finds 8 more trees there, 2 at Ofental and 1 at Robson Creek, and adds 7 to 11 spurious trees on each of those three plots, so F1 at 3 m does not improve (−0.001 to −0.014). It is the setting to change for a stand known to lean, not a better default.

    The height prior is the canopy's height. height_prior scales each tree's costs by one over the 95th percentile of the heights within 1.5 m of its stem. In a closed stand that is the height of whatever stands over the stem: at Robson Creek it is within 20 % of the height the tree ends up with for 22 of 352 candidates, and the candidates that end below 3 m had competed as trees of 25.7 m (11.1 m at Litchfield, 18.4 m at Wytham, 16.0 m at Ofental). Taking each tree's own height from a first pass and segmenting again helps where short trees stand under tall ones of one species and hurts where everything is tall and interlocked: F1 +0.070 at Ofental, −0.033 at Robson Creek, −0.006 at Wytham and +0.001 at Litchfield. It was not adopted.

    merge_branches is not free. The guide and the notebooks run it before segmenting; the sylva trees command does not. With the 3 cm graph it removes a fifth to two fifths of the spurious trees on every plot (77 → 60 at Robson Creek, 42 → 30 at Ofental), and it merges real trees wherever stems share a base: 22 of the 149 trees found at Wytham, a coppice (F1 0.861 → 0.798), 5 at each of Robson Creek and Ofental, none at Litchfield. F1 at 3 m moves by +0.002, +0.016, −0.062 and +0.005. The trees that remain also take more of the understorey (understorey taken rises by 0.05 to 0.19). It is a step for single-stemmed trees with low limbs, not for coppice or multi-stemmed stands.

    min_arc_deg failed real stems on thinned clouds, and was fixed. The arc was the longest run of occupied 10° bins. On these clouds (points about 2.5 cm apart) a stem of 5 cm radius has about three points a bin, a bin is empty by chance one time in twenty, and an unbroken 130° needs thirteen in a row: at Litchfield 9 % of well-supported stems under 5 cm radius passed, and 130° kept 11 % of the plot's trees (8 % at Robson Creek, 36 % at Wytham, 35 % at Ofental). The arc is now carried across the empty bins that the point count explains, up to two in a row. With that, 130° keeps 80 %, 56 %, 66 % and 65 % of the reference trees against 98 %, 74 %, 83 % and 71 % without it, at a precision of 0.97, 0.61, 0.90 and 0.73 against 0.98, 0.59, 0.90 and 0.60. What it still removes are stems that the scans did not see far enough round. It pays at Ofental (F1 0.649 → 0.688) and costs elsewhere (−0.10, −0.07 and −0.10).

    Measured and not adopted. Giving a seed point that two stems claim to the nearer bark instead of the better-supported stem (17 to 33 % of seed points go to a stem other than the nearest): more spurious trees on 14 of 16 runs, F1 −0.029 at Wytham and −0.028 at Ofental. Segmenting a second time from the candidates that survive pruning: +0.006 overall, 7 runs up and 7 down. The 99th height percentile in place of the top point, and CSF ground in place of PMF: no difference beyond the run-to-run range.

    The angle penalty and how well the plot is sampled¶

    This section is about the edge cost of versions up to 1.1.0, which the present cost replaced; angle_rate is no longer a setting. What it found, that the best cost depends on how completely the trees are sampled, holds for the present cost too.

    In those versions the cost of an edge rose with its angle from vertical as exp(angle_rate × degrees). The rate had been 0.046 since the method was written (a horizontal step costs 63 times a vertical one). Measured in November 2026 with paired runs, the best rate turns out to depend on how completely the trees are sampled:

    Data 0.046 (default) 0.023 0.012
    Litchfield (7 runs) 0.976 0.974 0.974
    Robson Creek (7 runs) 0.653 0.676 0.677
    Wytham (7 runs) 0.852 0.860 0.862
    Ofental (7 runs) 0.641 0.674 0.718
    Gingin banksia woodland, 291 trees (2 runs) 0.671 0.693 0.704
    Great Western Woodlands, 16 trees (2 runs) 0.660 0.690 0.698
    the four reference plots thinned to 5 cm (mean, 3 runs each) 0.765 0.777 0.754
    the same thinned to 8 cm, graph at 8 cm 0.612 0.608 0.566
    eight simulated plots, five scan positions each 0.721 0.710 0.693

    F1 with trees under 3 m pruned. The two woodland plots were never used to choose a setting. The simulated plots are four stand types of sylva.synthetic.plot, two seeds each, scanned with the VZ-400 model.

    At their native spacing of about 2.5 cm, every real plot but Litchfield gains from a lower rate, with fewer spurious trees (77 → 61 at Robson Creek and 43 → 33 at Ofental at 0.012), the same or a higher overlap of the trees found, and no more understorey taken. The mechanism is the one the miss analysis points to: with horizontal steps at 63 times the cost of vertical ones, the way out along a branch is so dear that a tree standing under it reaches the outer part more cheaply across a small vertical gap, and takes it.

    The simulated plots and the thinned clouds go the other way, and for a reason that can be measured. In the real plots 91 to 98 % of a tree's points are connected to its own base through its own points by links of 5 cm, and nearly all of them by links of 8 cm. In the simulated scans it is 22 to 45 % at 5 cm and 55 to 81 % at 8 cm: seen from five positions, the crowns are in pieces. A path then has to jump from piece to piece, the length term can no longer tell a tree's own gap from the gap to its neighbour, and the preference for what stands above the stem is what is left to hold a tree together.

    So the default was left unchanged, and angle_rate made a setting: 0.023 for plots scanned from many positions and kept at 2 to 5 cm, where it gained up to 0.03 and lost nothing beyond the run-to-run range; the default for sparse, coarse or heavily occluded clouds. Raising power behaves the same way (10 in place of 6: +0.012 on the reference plots, +0.02 at Gingin, −0.030 on the simulated ones) and was left alone for the same reason.

    Also measured in this round and not adopted, each as paired runs: a 4 cm clustering cell in stem detection (+0.011 on the reference plots, −0.08 at Gingin, where it adds a hundred spurious trees); 500 RANSAC iterations (+0.010, but 250, 1000 and 2000 give +0.003, +0.003 and −0.005: a different random draw, not a better fit); linking stem circles across layers where a fit failed (13 more trees found over the four plots, 16 to 25 more spurious); merging only the stems that join above breast height (joins of coppice stems and of fragments happen at the same heights); a higher lean limit alone; a 2 cm graph; and every other graph, seed, prior and pruning setting, none of which beat the defaults by more than the run-to-run range. Removing by hand every stem that the reference shows to be a fragment of another tree raised F1 by 0.02 to 0.05 on the three closed-canopy plots and recovered no missed tree, which bounds what better candidate filtering could do.

    Two other ways of dividing contested crowns were prototyped and rejected, both on the reference plots themselves:

    • A capacity from the pipe model. A stem can carry a crown in proportion to its cross-section, so a tree claiming far more than its DBH allows was costed up and the plot segmented again. On well-fitted stems crown size does go as DBH to the power 1.9 to 2.2 on three plots, and the trees that take a neighbour's branches sit at about twice the usual ratio; but the scatter of the relation is as large as that, and at Robson Creek the power is 0.8 to 1.3. Every strength tried lost F1: −0.04 to −0.09 with a fixed DBH² law, −0.06 to no change with the plot's own fitted law and a dead zone, and worse the harder it was applied.
    • Assignment by random walks. Each point goes to the stem a random walk from it most probably reaches first (Grady 2006), so a thick attachment should outweigh a thin contact. As a replacement for the cheapest path it scored 0.87 to 0.93 at Litchfield and 0.12 to 0.15 at Ofental: under a closed canopy the branches of neighbours touch at every height, and a few metres above the stem bases the probabilities blend (the best stem's averaged 0.15 at Ofental). Used only to re-decide a band around the cheapest-path boundaries it did less harm the narrower the band (−0.12 to −0.02), and in the narrowest it still cost Robson Creek 0.020 in each of four runs.

    The edge cost after version 1.1.0¶

    After version 1.1.0 the edge cost (the angle penalty of the section above) and the arc of a stem circle (until then counted in ten-degree bins) were replaced. An edge of length d now costs d ** power / max(bend_floor, cos) ** bend_power, the cosine being that of the angle between the edge and a direction that is the vertical turned by path_weight towards the way the path arrived: the form of raycloudtools' cost for bending a path (Lowe and Stepanas 2021; Devereux et al. 2026), which measures from the path alone. The arc runs from point to point while neighbours are within 15° of each other, each point standing for a sector of 10°.

    The defaults are bend_power=4, bend_floor=0.4 and path_weight=0.3: a turn across costs at most 39 times going straight on. F1 with trees under 3 m pruned:

    Data version 1.1.0 present default bend_power=2, bend_floor=0.3
    Litchfield (4 runs) 0.977 0.977 0.969
    Robson Creek (4 runs) 0.653 0.665 0.679
    Wytham (4 runs) 0.861 0.856 0.859
    Ofental (4 runs) 0.649 0.671 0.698
    Gingin banksia woodland (2 runs) 0.671 0.674 0.685
    Great Western Woodlands (2 runs) 0.660 0.652 0.690
    the four reference plots thinned to 5 cm (mean, 3 runs each) 0.765 0.775 0.778
    the same thinned to 8 cm, graph at 8 cm 0.612 0.609 0.600
    eight simulated plots, five scan positions each 0.721 0.719 0.706

    Over seven paired runs of each reference plot the present default differs from version 1.1.0 by +0.008 on average (+0.001 at Litchfield, +0.010 at Robson Creek, +0.001 at Wytham and +0.021 at Ofental), with three more trees found and six fewer spurious trees a run; on the 5 cm thinnings by +0.010, on the 8 cm thinnings by −0.002 and on the simulated plots by −0.002. The understorey taken is unchanged. A floor of 0.355, which reproduces the 63-fold penalty of version 1.1.0, changes nothing anywhere; 0.45 gains as much on the reference plots and loses 0.009 on the simulated ones.

    The gentler cost of the last column (a turn costs at most 11 times) does what the lower angle_rate did, for the same reason: it gains where trees are sampled completely (+0.02 on the reference plots, with 18 fewer spurious trees a run, and on both woodland plots) and loses where crowns are in pieces (−0.017 on the simulated plots, −0.005 at 8 cm). It is a setting, not the default.

    Following the path alone, as raycloudtools does (path_weight=1 with no floor), was measured with a prototype before the defaults were fixed: +0.023 on the reference plots and +0.05 at Gingin, −0.09 on the thinned clouds and −0.06 on the simulated plots. Where a tree is in pieces, the direction of a path that has just jumped a gap says little, and the vertical is what is left to hold the tree together.

    A first version also took the covered share of the circle from the gaps between points. It halved the stem candidates on the thinned clouds (F1 −0.05 at 5 cm and −0.22 at 8 cm) while leaving the native clouds where they were: a stem slice of a dozen points has no two neighbours within 15°. The covered share is the detector's own measure, the share of ten-degree bins that hold a point, and is unchanged.

    Merging trees that touch¶

    trees.merge_touching runs after segmentation. Detection favours recall, and a candidate that is really part of another tree (a second circle on a wide trunk, a low fork, a whorl of branches fitted as a stem) is grown like any other stem and comes away with a piece of its parent's crown. The oracle test above put the ceiling of removing such stems at 0.02 to 0.05 F1; what was missing was a way to know them without the reference. Their piece of crown gives them away: it lies against the parent along much of its extent, where two separate trees touch only at the edge of their crowns. The step thins the labelled points above 0.5 m to 5 cm, takes for each pair of trees the share of the smaller one's points that have a point of the larger within 10 cm, and merges the smaller into the larger while that share reaches a threshold, largest share first. Only trees that reach 3 m take part.

    Each figure is F1 with trees under 3 m pruned, as paired runs against the same pipeline without the step:

    Ofental (7) 0.641 0.648 0.659 0.688

    At 0.10, the default, no run on any data set is below the same run without the step, and no reference tree is lost in any of them: the gain is spurious trees removed, 77.3 → 76.6 at Robson Creek, 18.9 → 16.1 at Wytham, 42.7 → 40.7 at Ofental and 194 → 174 at Gingin, by merging two to seven trees a plot. The trees found and the share of the understorey that trees take are unchanged in every one of the 28 reference runs, and the overlap of the found trees with the reference is the same or higher. The two woodland plots were never used to choose a setting. The step takes one to three seconds on these plots.

    Lower thresholds remove more (at 0.06, 12 of Ofental's 43 spurious trees and 46 of Gingin's 194) and begin to cost real trees where the sampling is coarse: on the Litchfield cloud thinned to 5 cm one run of three loses a tree at 0.08 and two at 0.06, one of them a tree whose stem had been followed through ten layers, merged into a neighbour 1.6 m away with which its crown interlocks. On clouds at 2 to 3 cm, 0.06 lost no tree in any run and is worth trying where spurious trees matter more than the last tree.

    Two things were learned on the way. Without the 3 m rule the step merged low clumps into the trees they stood against, which the tree scores did not see and the understorey figure did (+0.01 to +0.07 of the points below 2 m on no reference tree); both trees of a pair must reach the height for it to stay where it was. And unlike merge_branches, which joins the stems of a coppice stool, this step left them apart: no Wytham tree was lost at any of the three thresholds at the plot's own density.

    What the October 2026 changes did¶

    Three things changed together in the segmentation, and were measured on all four plots with this protocol:

    1. The graph is bridged. A k-nearest-neighbour graph over thinned points left crowns disconnected from their trunks wherever a few sparsely sampled branches carried the connection, and which crowns depended on which point each voxel happened to keep. Moving the thinning grid by a fraction of a voxel moved F1 by -0.047 at Litchfield, +0.027 at Robson, +0.008 at Wytham and -0.014 at Ofental: more than any setting ever tuned here. The graph now joins its components wherever two come within max_edge, one edge per pair of components, so its connectivity is the cloud's at that distance and not the thinning's. (The division of contested crowns still depends on the grid; see One run is not a measurement.) On a synthetic plot of interlocking crowns that took the share of tree points left unlabelled from 31 % to under 1 %.
    2. Stems are seeded along the whole bark shell from the ground to half a metre above seed_height (1.5 m by default, so to 2 m), and the ground cut dropped from 0.25 m to 0.1 m (0 with a ground classification), so a trunk is labelled to within a hand of the ground. Neither changed a single tree's outcome once the grid was held still; what the earlier cut was protecting against turned out to be the grid artefact above.
    3. The understorey sources are off by default. Costed as a 10 m tree, or by the vegetation height around each source, or by the height of the column above it up to the first gap, they found no tree on any plot and lost two at Litchfield and four at Wytham. What they take from a crown they take through foliage from below, whatever they are costed. (That judged them on trees alone; what they keep out of the trees is measured above.)

    Scored the way the benchmark scores¶

    Cherlet et al. (2026) evaluate only the trees that lie at least 90 % inside the test sub-plot (128, 181, 89 and 150 of them, which is what the in_plot_th0.90 folders hold), and a prediction whose best overlap is with an edge tree is neglected rather than counted against precision. Matching is Hungarian on the IoU matrix, true positive at IoU ≥ 0.5. Scoring against every labelled instance instead, and calling every edge-tree hit a false positive, makes the numbers look far worse than they are.

    Scored with a port of the benchmark's own evaluation code (qforestlab/TreeInstSegEval: IoU on point sets rounded to 1 cm, Hungarian matching on the evaluated trees, a true positive at IoU > 0.5), on the test splits, with min_height set to the 5 m the references themselves use (6 m at Robson Creek; prune_trees keeps 3 m by default). The figures an earlier version of this page gave (0.976, 0.732, 0.667, 0.528) could not be reproduced with that code and are superseded. The table below is the September 2026 record with the defaults of that time (power=6, a 25 cm cut, understorey sources on, an unbridged graph); the test-split data it needs is no longer on the machine the benchmarks run on, so it has not been re-scored after the October changes:

    The other methods' values are those of Cherlet et al. (2026), Table 2 (Rayextract, Treeiso, SSSC, and TreeLearn and ForAINet fine-tuned on all four plots). Sylva's are computed here with the same protocol, so the comparison is indicative rather than independent.

    power (the exponent on graph edge length) was raised from 4 to 6 after a sweep on the benchmark's validation areas, never on the test splits: F1 there went 0.514 → 0.563 at Robson Creek, 0.636 → 0.660 at Ofental and 0.581 → 0.588 at Wytham, and Litchfield did not change. On the test splits, scored once with the setting fixed, it gave 0.479 → 0.494, 0.682 → 0.788, 0.707 → 0.726 and 0.964 → 0.964. Settings that did not help on the validation areas: gravity, wood_costs (wood and leaf factors on the edge cost, an option up to version 1.1.0; better at Ofental only), a weaker or no height_prior, and two forms of a crown-size prior from stem allometry (crown radius proportional to DBH, applied to every path or only to points two trees compete for), which truncated real crowns or fragmented trees.

    What remains. Stem detection is nearly complete (one of the 150 Robson Creek trees is never detected); the losses are in dividing crowns between neighbours. Most missed trees are shorter than their neighbours and are absorbed by them: 49 of 76 at Robson Creek, 44 of 57 at Wytham and 21 of 31 at Ofental before the change of power.

    The reporting threshold matters more than anything else we tuned, because the references only label trees: at Litchfield, moving it from 3 m to 5 m took F1 from 0.810 to 0.976 under the earlier scoring, without losing a single tree, since everything it drops is shrub. Keep the threshold you segment with separate from the one you report, and set the second to match whatever your inventory calls a tree.

    Seeds used to be too wide. A tree starts from the points within seed_radius of its stem at seed_height; at 0.5 m those discs overlap wherever stems are close, and the stronger seed takes the other's crown. Of the 102 Wytham trees missed before, 79 had been swallowed by a neighbour whose stem stood a median of 0.3 m away: coppice stools and low forks, not distant trees. Halving seed_radius to 0.25 m and merge_radius to 0.2 m finds 22 more trees (130 → 152), lifts mean IoU 0.83 → 0.85 and precision 0.88 → 0.90, and costs Litchfield nothing (135 → 136).

    A sparser graph and a steeper cost help on top of that: k 10 → 6 and power 3 → 4 (the exponent on edge length, so a path pays more for a long hop between two crowns; since raised to 6, above). Found trees: Litchfield 136, Wytham 152 → 154, Robson 105 → 110, Ofental 63 → 68, with fewer spurious trees everywhere.

    A finer graph pays for itself where the stand is dense: the cloud is thinned to voxel_size before the graph is built, and 0.05 → 0.03 m gives Wytham 154 → 156, Robson 110 → 117 and Ofental 68 → 72, each with fewer false trees and a better IoU, for about 1.7× the time. Litchfield does not move at any resolution. 0.02 m gains two more at Wytham for 2.5× the time, which did not seem worth making the default.

    Other things tried, none of which helped: a denser graph (k=20, Wytham 136), gravity 0.3 (140), wood costs (142), dropping the height prior (139 at Wytham, 61 at Ofental), a wider height-prior radius (62 at Ofental), no angle penalty (48 at Ofental, much worse), max_edge 0.5 (no change), and no pruning at all (142), so the limit is not the candidate list.

    Where the remaining loss is. Robson and Ofental are still the hard ones, and for different reasons. At Ofental 41 of the 54 missed trees are absorbed by one neighbour, and the missed trees are short (median 10.1 m) where the matched ones are tall (16.4 m): suppressed conifers under dominants, whose stems are detected (81 % have a candidate within 0.5 m, median 0.05 m) but whose crowns are taken by the tree above. Robson keeps 222 reference trees against 655 candidates, so it over-detects in the understorey as well as merging. Both point at the cost model rather than at detection or pruning.

    Of Robson's 212 spurious trees, 123 sit almost entirely (> 80 % of their points) on what the reference calls ground (rainforest understorey, median 6.2 m tall), and only 40 are fragments of a labelled tree. The same tension as the savanna shrubs: real vegetation the benchmark does not count as a tree. prune_trees(min_quality_short=0.15) trims about a quarter of them for two found trees.

    Two ideas from other systems were implemented and measured, and both made things worse on this data:

    • A lateral-offset prior. raycloudtools multiplies every edge by 1 + g·(horizontal offset from the seed)², which is how it stops a dominant reaching sideways into a neighbour. Sylva has it as gravity. Ofental barely moves (F1 0.667 → 0.674 at g = 1, with mean IoU falling 0.718 → 0.694) and Robson falls apart: 0.521 → 0.487 → 0.450 → 0.437 for g = 0.1, 0.3, 1. In raycloudtools the term multiplies a cost already normalised by canopy height; here it stacks on length⁴, so a 10 m-wide rainforest crown pays about ×31 on every edge.
    • Edge weight as the gap between clusters rather than the distance travelled. This is TLS2trees' (Wilkes et al. 2023) documented fix for a path that prefers a suppressed tree's base. Grouping points into 0.25 m cubes and pricing each edge by the closest approach between two groups gives Ofental F1 0.667 → 0.158 and Robson 0.521 → 0.150. The reason is instructive: with gap weights, groups that touch cost nothing, so in a closed canopy the whole plot becomes one cheap component and a single seed takes it. TLS2trees avoids this by running the gap graph on wood points only, with clusters from 0.2 m slices, and attaching foliage afterwards from the branch tips of the finished skeletons. Done that way it might work; done naively it does not, and the code was removed rather than shipped as a knob.
